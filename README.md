# Garmin Venu Care: a brand extension proposal

Author: Saathvik Maheshuni

This is my midterm paper for Marketing at NYU Tandon (fall 2026, Professor Jamyn Edis). The assignment was to propose one new product under a brand that already exists, aimed at a customer that brand doesn't fully serve yet, and to argue for it with what we covered in class: the customer, the brand, the channel and the price.

I proposed Garmin Venu Care. It's a Venu-class watch with LTE, all-day fall detection and a staffed 24/7 response plan, for older adults who live alone and have already fallen, and for the adult children who worry about them. Venu Care is my own idea, not a real Garmin product.

## What the paper argues

- **Market size:** 16.2 million adults 65 and older live alone in the US ($12.47 billion of first-year spending at my price). After cutting for people who fell in the past year, a rough poverty screen and people who already use a medical alert device, that leaves 2.95 million people ($2.27 billion). I built Year 1 from the bottom up, from a media budget and Best Buy shelf space: 36,000 plans, or 1.22% of that group.
- **Positioning:** what sets it apart is a week between charges. A watch on its charger is off the wrist, and only 18% of medical alert users wear theirs all the time.
- **Price:** $349.99 for the watch plus $34.99 a month. I used perceived-value pricing, under a first-year ceiling I estimated at about $850.
- **Channel:** a US launch on October 1, 2027 through garmin.com and a trial in 300 Best Buy stores.
- **Money:** Year 1 brings $18.4 million of revenue and $7.0 million of contribution, which ends as a $1.8 million loss after $8.8 million of spending to get customers. Lifetime value is 3.15 times the cost to get a customer, or 2.39 on the HBS calculator at a 12% discount rate.
- **The case against:** the strongest one is a $299 cellular Apple Watch SE 3. My rule for stopping is 90-day paid retention below 90% for the first customers.
